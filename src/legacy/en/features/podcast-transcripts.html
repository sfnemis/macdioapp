Feature

Every episode, in writing.

Apple's speech recognition runs on the device in your hand and turns the episode into text you can read, search and jump through. The audio never leaves the device.

As of 12 October 2026 Macdio makes a transcript for any podcast episode on the iPhone, iPad or Mac that plays it, using Apple's on-device speech recognition, in the episode's language where the device supports it. The audio is not uploaded, there is no account and no server of mine. When a show publishes its own transcript, Macdio uses that instead. The transcript follows the audio line by line, is searchable across your whole library, and is the source for the chapters, the summary and the song list of the episode.

How it works

Press play, or ask for the transcript from the episode. Macdio decodes the audio and hands it to Apple's speech recognition on the device. The text is saved with the episode and synced through your iCloud, so an episode transcribed on the Mac is already in text on the iPhone. On Apple TV the transcripts made on your other devices arrive through iCloud, since the TV cannot run the recognition itself.

Publisher transcripts first

Some shows ship a transcript in the feed, in SRT, WebVTT, JSON, HTML or plain text. Macdio reads those, shows them in the same panel and follows them during playback, so a professionally transcribed show keeps the names and spellings its producers chose.

What the text is used for

  • Search: a word typed in Podcasts finds every sentence that contains it, across all transcripts, and plays from that second.
  • Chapters: publisher chapters when they exist, generated ones from the transcript when they do not.
  • Summary: a short written summary of the episode, made from the transcript by Apple's language model, on the device where it can run and through Apple's Private Cloud Compute on OS 27.
  • Highlights: a marked moment saves the sentence being spoken, not only the timestamp. How highlights work
  • Songs: a music podcast's songs are identified from the audio and listed with the time they play.

Languages

Transcription runs in the language the episode is in, when the device supports that language on its own. The set depends on the device and OS version; the app lists what is available. A show in a language the device cannot transcribe still plays normally and still shows a publisher transcript if there is one.

Common questions

Does Macdio upload the audio to make a transcript?
No. Apple's speech recognition runs on the iPhone, iPad or Mac itself. There is no server and no account.
Does it work for every podcast?
For every episode in a language the device supports for on-device recognition. Shows that publish their own transcript use that one.
Does it work on Apple TV?
The Apple TV shows the transcripts, chapters and song lists that your iPhone, iPad or Mac made, synced through iCloud. It cannot run the recognition itself.
Can I search inside episodes?
Yes. Search in Podcasts looks through every transcript in your library and plays the episode from the sentence you pick.
Does this cost extra?
No. Transcripts are part of the one-time purchase.
