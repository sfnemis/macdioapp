Four ways that work, in the order I would try them, plus what to do when a stream you love goes quiet.

A station's stream URL is the direct address of its audio, and there are four reliable ways to get it: the station's own listen page, an open community directory, the Network tab of your browser's developer tools, and simply asking the station. They are below in the order worth trying, along with what to do when an address that used to work goes quiet.

## What a stream URL is

It is the direct address of the audio feed rather than the page around it. Some end in a file extension like .mp3, .aac or .m3u8, others are a bare host and port. Paste one into a player and the station starts, with no website in between.

## 1. The station's own website

Start here, because it is the source. Many stations publish a listen or tune-in page offering an .m3u or .pls file, often labelled for VLC, Winamp or "other players". Those small files are playlists that contain the real stream address, and any decent player reads them directly.

## 2. An open community directory

Community-maintained databases collect stream addresses that listeners have submitted and that get checked automatically. This is where most radio apps, including Macdio, get their catalog, and it is usually the fastest way to find a station whose site is hard to navigate or in a language you do not read. Once you have an address, [how to listen to internet radio on a Mac](/guides/how-to-listen-to-radio-on-mac.html) covers what to paste it into.

## 3. Browser developer tools

If a station only offers a web player, the address is still visible. Open the player page, open the browser's developer tools, choose the Network tab, filter to Media, and press play. The long-running request that keeps growing is the stream. Right click it and copy the URL.

## 4. Ask the station

Underrated and often the quickest. Small stations answer email, and many are happy that someone wants to listen properly. It also avoids the guesswork when a station runs several different quality feeds.

## A word on what you may do with it

Finding a public stream address and listening to it yourself is ordinary listening. Redistributing a station's audio, rebroadcasting it, or building a service around someone else's feed is a different matter and carries its own licensing obligations. Keep it personal and keep it simple: point your own player at the station and enjoy it.

## When a stream goes silent

Stations move servers more often than people expect. A dead URL rarely means a dead station, so look the station up again before giving up; there is usually a new address, and it is worth updating the custom station rather than deleting it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a radio stream URL?

It is the direct address of a station's audio feed rather than the page around it. Some end in .mp3, .aac or .m3u8, others are a bare host and port, and a player opens them without any website in between.

### How do I find the stream URL of a station?

Try the station's own listen page first, then an open community directory, then the Network tab of your browser's developer tools while the web player runs. If none of those work, ask the station by email.

### Why does a stream URL stop working?

Stations change servers more often than listeners expect. A dead address usually means a moved stream rather than a closed station, so look the station up again and update the URL instead of deleting it.
