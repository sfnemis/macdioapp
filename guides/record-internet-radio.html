Record internet radio, song by song.

Taping the radio is as old as radio itself. Here is how it works on a Mac and an iPhone today, and how a whole show turns into named, individual tracks.

Yes, internet radio can be recorded, and on a Mac or an iPhone it takes one tap in a player that supports it. The recording is the stream itself rather than whatever the speakers happen to be playing, so it stays clean and keeps the broadcast's own quality. What follows is how it works on each device, how large the files get, and where they live afterwards.

Is recording radio allowed?

Recording a broadcast for your own personal, private listening is a long-standing practice in most countries, the same tradition as taping FM onto a cassette. What stays personal stays simple: do not redistribute or re-publish recordings, and check your own country's rules if unsure.

The hard way

On a Mac, audio-capture tools can record whatever the system plays. If you are still choosing how to play radio there, start with how to listen to internet radio on a Mac. They work, but they capture one long file with no idea where a song starts or ends, and notification sounds land in the recording too. On an iPhone, system audio capture is not available to apps at all, so this route does not exist.

The one-tap way

Macdio records the stream itself rather than the speaker output, on both Mac and iPhone. Tap the record button while listening and the audio is saved in the stream's original quality, clean of any system sounds. Two modes:

Continuous

How to record internet radio on a Mac

In Macdio, start the station and click record. The audio is written as it arrives, so nothing is re-encoded and no system sounds leak in. Split by Track mode watches the song information coming from the station and cuts the session at each change, so a two hour show becomes a list of named tracks with artwork instead of one long file you have to scrub through.

How to record radio on an iPhone

The same button, the same result. Tap record while a station plays and the recording continues while you use other apps, because it is the stream being written rather than the microphone listening. Recordings appear in the Recordings tab as soon as you stop.

How large do the files get?

A recording is as heavy as the stream, since nothing is added or compressed further. At 128 kbps, the most common bitrate, that is roughly 58 MB an hour, so a full evening of radio is about 350 MB. A 320 kbps station is around 2.4 MB a minute, and a low bitrate talk station can be a quarter of that. Split by Track makes the arithmetic friendlier in practice, because you keep the three songs you wanted and delete the rest.

Where recordings live

On your own device, in the Recordings tab, where each session and each split track can be played or deleted. They stay inside the app rather than landing in your music library or the Files app, which keeps a personal recording personal.

Frequently asked questions

What quality are the recordings?
The stream's own quality. Macdio saves the audio exactly as the station broadcasts it, with no re-encoding loss.
Where do recordings live?
Locally on your device, listed in the Recordings tab, where each track can be played or deleted. They stay inside the app.
What format are the recordings?
Whatever the station broadcasts, usually MP3 or AAC. Macdio writes the stream's own bytes, so nothing is converted and no quality is lost on the way to disk.
Can I record while using other apps?
Yes. Recording follows playback, so it continues while you switch apps or lock the phone.
Can I record on Apple Watch or Apple TV?
Recording is a Mac and iPhone feature. Favorites and saved songs still sync everywhere.
