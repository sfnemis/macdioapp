Record internet radio, song by song.

Taping the radio is as old as radio itself. Here is how it works on a Mac and an iPhone today, and how a whole show turns into named, individual tracks.

Is recording radio allowed?

Recording a broadcast for your own personal, private listening is a long-standing practice in most countries, the same tradition as taping FM onto a cassette. What stays personal stays simple: do not redistribute or re-publish recordings, and check your own country's rules if unsure.

The hard way

On a Mac, audio-capture tools can record whatever the system plays. They work, but they capture one long file with no idea where a song starts or ends, and notification sounds land in the recording too. On an iPhone, system audio capture is not available to apps at all, so this route does not exist.

The one-tap way

Macdio records the stream itself rather than the speaker output, on both Mac and iPhone. Tap the record button while listening and the audio is saved in the stream's original quality, clean of any system sounds. Two modes:

Continuous

One file for the whole session, exactly as broadcast. Ideal for shows, interviews and DJ sets.

Split by Track

Macdio watches the station's song data and cuts the session into individual tracks, each named and carrying its own artwork.

Yours to keep

Recordings stay on your device, playable in the app and exportable any time. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

What quality are the recordings?
The stream's own quality. Macdio saves the audio exactly as the station broadcasts it, with no re-encoding loss.
Where do recordings live?
Locally on your device, listed in the Recordings tab, with per-track play, delete and export.
Can I record on Apple Watch or Apple TV?
Recording is a Mac and iPhone feature. Favorites and saved songs still sync everywhere.
