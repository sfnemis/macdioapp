Does the iPhone have an FM radio?

The short answer is no, and it is not coming. Here is the honest version of why, and what listeners do instead.

The short answer

There is no FM radio you can switch on in an iPhone. Apple has said publicly that its phones have no FM chip that can be enabled, and newer models also lack the antenna such a chip would need. No app can change that, because the hardware access simply is not there. Any app calling itself an FM radio for iPhone is an internet radio player.

Which iPhone models have FM radio?

None of them. Not the first iPhone, not the iPhone 17 generation, and nothing in between. The question comes back every September because each new model brings a new round of searches, but the answer has never changed. In 2017 Apple stated plainly that its phones have no FM radio chip and no antenna designed to support FM signals, so there is no ability to enable FM reception in them. Older models did carry combination wireless chips that happened to include an FM tuner block on the silicon, but that block was never wired to an antenna and was never exposed to software. A dormant transistor is not a radio.

Why people keep asking

Because plenty of Android phones did ship with a usable FM receiver, so anyone who has seen it work on one phone reasonably assumes every phone can do it. The question also carries a real concern underneath it, which is what happens when the network goes down. That one deserves its own answer, further down this page.

Receiver or transmitter? They are not the same thing

These two get mixed up constantly, and the distinction matters because one of them has a real workaround.

An FM receiver picks up broadcasts. No iPhone has a usable one, for the reasons above.

An FM transmitter does the opposite: it broadcasts a short-range signal that a nearby radio can tune into. No iPhone has ever had one either. This is the one people can actually solve, though, and the fix is an accessory rather than an app. A car FM transmitter takes audio from the phone over Bluetooth or a cable and rebroadcasts it on an empty frequency, which is how older cars without CarPlay or an aux input end up playing whatever the phone is streaming. Anything on the App Store calling itself an FM transmitter is either controlling such an accessory or misusing the term.

What happened to the headphone antenna trick

On many Android phones, FM reception only worked with wired headphones plugged in, because the cable doubled as the antenna. That is the source of a lot of the confusion, since people who saw it work on one phone reasonably assume every phone can do it. It was never an iPhone feature, and the iPhone has not had a headphone jack since the iPhone 7 in 2016, so even the physical prerequisite is gone.

The emergency argument, taken seriously

The strongest case for FM is not music, it is what happens when the network fails. A broadcast signal keeps arriving when cell towers are congested, powerless or destroyed, and that is exactly when people want information. This argument deserves a straight answer rather than a sales pitch: if that is the risk you are planning for, buy a cheap battery or hand-crank FM radio and keep it in a drawer. No streaming app substitutes for it, this one included.

What the iPhone does offer on that front is a different set of tools. Wireless Emergency Alerts arrive over the cellular network without any app, and recent models can send an SOS message by satellite when there is no cellular coverage at all. Useful, but a complement to a broadcast radio rather than a replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Is there an FM radio on an iPhone?
No. Apple has stated the iPhone has no FM chip that can be enabled, and modern models have no FM antenna. Radio on iPhone means internet streaming.
Can an app unlock FM on my iPhone?
No. The hardware access does not exist, so no app can provide FM reception. Apps advertising it are streaming over the internet.
Does the iPhone 17 have FM radio?
No. No iPhone generation, including the iPhone 17 line, ships with FM radio reception. The answer has been the same for every model to date.
Do iPhones have a radio receiver?
Not a usable one. Some older iPhones carried a wireless chip that included an FM tuner block, but it was never connected to an antenna and was never available to software.
Do iPhones have an FM transmitter?
No. To broadcast phone audio to a nearby radio, such as a car stereo with no aux input, you need a separate FM transmitter accessory that connects over Bluetooth or a cable.
Does radio work in the car?
